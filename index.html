The Philosophy

Most retail traders are learning a version of trading that was never designed to make them successful.

They learn how to react to price — but never how to understand it. Here you learn what actually moves price — the macro, the flows behind it, and the discipline to trade it. That is where consistency comes from.

01

Most traders never learn what actually moves price

Price is driven by macroeconomics, liquidity, institutional positioning, interest rates and capital flows — not isolated patterns or random signals. The MAISON YR framework was built around understanding those deeper market dynamics first.

02

Technical analysis alone is not enough

Charts matter. But most retail traders learn technical analysis in isolation — patterns, indicators, setups — never how to connect them to the broader financial environment driving price underneath the surface. Here, technical execution is combined with macro, liquidity and structured reasoning.

03

Institutions leave footprints in the market

Banks, hedge funds and institutional participants cannot move massive amounts of capital invisibly. Their positioning impacts liquidity, volatility and long-term direction — leaving measurable footprints. Members learn to identify and understand those flows instead of emotionally reacting against them.

04

Consistency comes from structure

The biggest difference between profitable and unprofitable traders is rarely intelligence. It is structure. Risk management, emotional discipline, controlled execution and consistency under pressure separate long-term traders from short-term gamblers.

05

The goal is independence

Markets evolve. Conditions change. Narratives rotate. Traders who rely on external signals struggle the moment the environment shifts. MAISON YR develops traders capable of understanding markets independently — reasoning, structure, adaptability. The objective is not dependency. The objective is understanding.

None of this is talent. It is taught. It simply was never taught to you.

The Story

Football. Banking. Markets. MAISON YR.

A filmed version of this is in the cut. The written one is below, and it is the same story.

I started football at three years old. I left my parents' house at twelve to pursue it, and turned professional at sixteen. The game took me across the world — France, the Netherlands, Belgium, Greece, England, the United States.

I started trading at sixteen, while still playing. I joined a trading course in Belgium — in person, a full year. It was technical analysis only: charts, patterns, indicators. After that year, my account was up and down — mostly down. That failure taught me the most important lesson of my career: charts don't move markets. The macro does. Every market is correlated — stocks, forex, gold, oil, crypto — and there is always a reason price moves. The course never taught me the reason.

So I went to find it where it actually lives. I studied economics, earned an internship at a bank, and became an analyst — the person who reads everything impacting markets and feeds it up the chain to the traders. Then I became a trader for the bank. Then I left to trade my own capital.

Since then, trading has changed my life. I've generated over $5M in trading profits — and I've never touched that capital. Not once. My capital compounds toward one goal — generational wealth — and my lifestyle is funded elsewhere. Never from the trading account.

In 2023, still playing professionally, I was diagnosed with arthritis in my ankle. Doctors told me to stop. I didn't listen. Five surgeries later, the ankle still hurts, the arthritis isn't leaving, and the level I played at isn't coming back. Last year, I retired. Football had been my life since I was three years old. Losing it left a hole.

Then a major US trading platform reached out and asked me to become a mentor. I started sharing my trades there — and understood something quickly: giving people trades will never make them independent. Signals don't build skill. What builds skill is the reality of trading — how banks trade, how institutions trade: fundamentals first, macro first, knowing why price moves before you ever touch an entry. That became the new passion.

Seven people came in before there was a house to put them in. I worked with those seven one-on-one, around the clock, for months — while building the platform that could carry the standard without me standing over it. MAISON YR opened publicly in April 2026, and the record went public with it. Then I closed the door again: not because it wasn't working — the quality was there from day one — but because there was still no structure to scale it.

Now it can — and the door is open. The goal has never changed: make you financially free and independent in your trading. Teach you to fish. That's the house.

— Yanis Rucquois · Founder, MAISON YR
